FlexGaussian: Flexible and Cost-Effective Training-Free Compression for 3D Gaussian Splatting

Abstract

3D Gaussian splatting has emerged as a powerful technique for representing and rendering complex 3D scenes, offering high fidelity and real-time performance. However, as model sizes grow, efficient compression becomes essential—particularly for deployment on mobile and edge devices with constrained memory and compute resources. While existing methods can significantly reduce the size of 3D Gaussian models, they often rely on retraining or fine-tuning, making them less adaptable to varying compression requirements.

In this paper, we present FlexGaussian, a flexible, training-free compression framework that combines mixed-precision quantization with attribute-discriminative pruning. FlexGaussian requires no retraining and can easily adapt to different compression targets. Our method achieves up to 96.4% compression with minimal quality degradation (less than 1 dB PSNR drop) and is suitable for real-time rendering on mobile devices. Furthermore, FlexGaussian is 1.7×–2.1× faster than state-of-the-art training-free methods and 10×–100× faster than training-involved approaches, making it highly efficient for practical use.

Methodology

Problem Formulation

FlexGaussian aims to compress pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models without any access to training images or retraining. Each 3D-GS model can be viewed as an \( N \times M \) matrix, where each row represents a Gaussian and each column an attribute (e.g., position, rotation, scale, opacity, color, spherical harmonics). The goal is to reduce model size while preserving rendering quality within a user-specified threshold.

Channel-wise Mixed-Precision Quantization (MPQ)

Unlike uniform quantization, FlexGaussian observes that different attribute channels (e.g., position vs. spherical harmonics) have varying sensitivity to bit-width reduction. Based on this, it applies a channel-wise quantization strategy using INT4 or INT8 depending on each channel’s quality sensitivity. To further improve accuracy, attributes are divided into sub-groups, each quantized with its own dynamic range. This selective precision strategy enables substantial size reduction with minimal visual degradation.

Attribute-Discriminative Pruning (ADP)

FlexGaussian also introduces a semi-structured pruning scheme that targets redundant attributes rather than pruning entire Gaussians. Using a global significance score that captures the contribution of each Gaussian to rendering (considering opacity, volume, and pixel impact), it selectively removes low-impact attributes (e.g., spherical harmonics) from low-importance Gaussians. This yields a better trade-off between compression ratio and quality than prior row-based or attribute-only pruning.

Fast Online Adaptation (FOA)

To support real-time adaptability, FlexGaussian includes a Fast Online Adaptation module that efficiently explores compression configurations given a target quality or compression ratio. By leveraging the consistent structure of Pareto-optimal frontiers across scenes, FOA quickly searches for the best MPQ and ADP parameters without retraining, typically completing in seconds.

Evaluation

We evaluate FlexGaussian across three standard datasets—Mip-NeRF360, Tanks&Temples, and Deep Blending—covering a wide range of indoor and outdoor scenes with varying levels of complexity. Comparisons are made against the original 3DGS and multiple compression baselines, including the training-free method FCGS and several training-involved methods such as LightGaussian, Compressed3D, PUP-3DGS, and CompGS. Evaluation metrics include compression ratio, rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), and compression time. FlexGaussian achieves up to 96.4% compression with less than 1 dB PSNR drop, while requiring only seconds to run—demonstrating 1.7×–2.1× speedup over FCGS and up to 100× faster than retraining-based methods. Notably, FCGS fails on over half the scenes due to excessive memory usage, necessitating a reduced-memory variant for fair comparison. All experiments were conducted on an RTX 3090 setup, with additional deployment tested on the Jetson Xavier to validate FlexGaussian’s efficiency on edge devices..
